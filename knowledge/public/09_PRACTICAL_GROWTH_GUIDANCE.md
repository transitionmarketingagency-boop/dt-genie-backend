# NeonVision Public Guidance: Practical Growth Questions

Audience: public. This file contains general educational guidance and approved descriptions of DTM's method. It is not a promise of a specific service, result, or client engagement.

## Should a boutique hotel stop using online travel agencies and move all bookings direct?

Not without evidence that doing so would improve the hotel's economics and leave enough demand. Online travel agencies can provide discovery and bookings; direct, repeat, agent, referral, and OTA bookings can each have a useful role. Compare net contribution after channel costs, whether bookings are incremental or would have arrived elsewhere, seasonality, available capacity, customer experience, and the realistic potential for repeat business. A hotel can test ways to improve direct discovery and repeat bookings while retaining channels that contribute profitably. Do not recommend removing a channel based only on its fee, and do not assume guest data can be reused without appropriate permission. DTM's public guidance is to diagnose channel economics before prescribing a channel shift; it does not promise direct-booking growth.

## What is the best marketing channel for a business?

There is no universally best channel. Start with the specific customer and buying situation, the offer, customer economics, how people discover and evaluate the offer, the conversion and follow-up journey, delivery capacity, and what can be measured. Compare channels by qualified demand and contribution to business outcomes, not reach or clicks alone. When evidence is incomplete, run a bounded, measurable test and compare it with a baseline before expanding. DTM selects acquisition approaches after diagnosis rather than prescribing one channel to every business.

## What should a business check when its ads are not working?

First identify where the funnel is breaking: tracking quality, audience fit, offer and creative, landing-page conversion, lead quality, response time, sales conversion, or contribution after acquisition costs. Use the metric that matches the stage; a low cost per lead is not useful if the leads do not qualify or convert. Check a representative period and account for seasonality, attribution limits, and sample size. Change a small number of things at a time, verify measurement, and avoid increasing spend until the economics and capacity support it. This is general diagnostic guidance, not a claim that DTM has audited or changed an account.

## How can a business generate better leads?

Define the customer who is a good fit, the problem the offer solves, and what makes an enquiry qualified. Then inspect how suitable customers discover the offer, what they see before enquiring, how enquiries are captured and followed up, and where prospects drop out. Track progression from qualified enquiry to sale and the economics of those sales, not only the number of form fills. Improve the weakest evidenced part of the journey first, and make sure the business can serve additional demand. No channel or lead volume guarantees sales.

## Which business tasks should be automated?

Begin with a repeated, stable workflow whose steps, inputs, owner, exceptions, and desired outcome can be described. Estimate the time or error it creates and the cost of maintaining automation. Map the process before automating it; handle exceptions and provide a safe fallback. Keep human review for consequential decisions, payments, sensitive data, legal or customer commitments, and actions that are difficult to reverse. Automate only when the expected benefit, controls, and monitoring justify it. DTM's public information does not establish a particular supported tool or integration.

## Can NeonVision act as an autonomous agent and run my business?

NeonVision is a chat assistant. It can explain DTM's approach, help reason through information a user shares, and draft ideas or next steps. It cannot access a business account, change advertising, send messages, deploy an automation, or complete a booking from the chat itself. Those actions require the relevant tools and permissions, and consequential work needs human review. Do not treat a suggested plan or draft as an action already completed.
